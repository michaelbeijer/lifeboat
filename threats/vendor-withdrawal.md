# Vendor withdrawal

**A tool you depend on becomes unavailable — to you specifically, or in your country.**

## Causes

Export controls or sanctions. Regulatory action. Geo-restriction. Acquisition and shutdown. Price changes that put a tool out of reach. A change of terms you cannot accept.

Any single vendor disappearing is unlikely. Some vendor in your stack changing materially over five years is close to certain.

## What it takes

Rarely your data. Usually your *workflow* — the accumulated configuration, the integrations, the habits, and the specific outputs you have tuned around.

For a small business the disruption is measured in days of lost productivity rather than lost files, which is precisely why it gets no planning at all.

## Controls

**Know your exit before you need it.** For each significant tool: can you export everything, in a standard format, today, without a support ticket? If not, that is your answer about how replaceable it is.

**Prefer open formats and open protocols.** Data in files you can read with other software survives its vendor. Data in a proprietary container does not.

**Keep a rough second choice in mind** for each critical tool. Not configured, not paid for — just identified, so a forced switch starts from a decision rather than a research project.

**Separate the interface from the substance.** Where a tool is a front-end to something standardised, switching is cheap. Where it owns the format, it owns you.

**Watch for concentration.** Using one vendor for mail, storage, passwords, VPN and calendar is convenient and recreates in miniature exactly the single-vendor exposure you are trying to escape elsewhere.

## The realistic posture

You cannot prepare thoroughly for every vendor. You can:

- Keep exports current for the two or three that would hurt most
- Avoid formats you cannot read without the vendor
- Notice when a tool is becoming load-bearing, and check its exit before it becomes indispensable rather than after
